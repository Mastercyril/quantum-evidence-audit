We audit every IBM Quantum experiment record recoverable from a year of personal cloud storage: thirty job entries spanning December 2025 to July 2026 across six backends, with register widths from two to forty bits and shot counts from 1,024 to 4,096. Eight job entries survive as original Qiskit Runtime exports; eighteen exist only as transcribed counts in session reports; four could not be retrieved at all. The audit separates what the files establish from what the transcripts assert. Transcribed highlights, including an 86.9 percent Bell-state fidelity and an 80 percent GHZ-4 visibility, remain unverified because the underlying exports were not recovered. The frequently cited CHSH value S = 2.78 cannot be reconstructed: the recovered exports contain computational-basis populations only, with no rotated-basis correlation data and no recovered circuit definitions. The audit also documents four internal inconsistencies in the transcribed record, including shot totals that sum to 1,025 rather than 1,024 and a stated entropy that is incompatible with the stated peak. We argue the package is useful as a worked example of record decay, and we publish the inventory, the discrepancy log and a retention checklist so that future experiments are verifiable by default rather than reconstructable only in principle.
